# The Two Instructions
*A dialogue in four chapters*

At the end of a meeting, Thea found Damon standing before a board marked LAUNCH NOW. In his hand was a note from the security discussion: REVIEW FIRST.

**Thea:** You seem to have two orders.

**Damon:** I have an agent. I hoped it would tell me which one to obey.

**Thea:** Can it see both?

**Damon:** It can see nearly everything.

**Thea:** Then let us ask what seeing gives it.

## I. The doors

**Damon:** The agent is connected to the project board, the conversation, and the drive where the security review lives. It can find the launch plan, the warning, and the names of the people involved. Is this not what we wanted?

**Thea:** It is more than either of us could hold in mind at once. But what does REVIEW FIRST mean?

**Damon:** That we must review before launch.

**Thea:** Was it a decision?

**Damon:** It was said in the security discussion.

**Thea:** Was everything said there a decision?

**Damon:** No. Some people asked questions. One proposed delaying the launch.

**Thea:** And the note you hold?

**Damon:** I have not yet checked who wrote it, or whether the proposal was accepted.

**Thea:** Then your agent has brought you the right uncertainty. That is already useful. Would it be better if it concealed the conflict and chose the later sentence?

**Damon:** No. The later sentence might have been a suggestion. But the board might be stale.

**Thea:** So access lets the agent bring the witnesses together. It does not, by itself, appoint one witness judge over the others.

**Damon:** Yet if the conversation plainly recorded an approved decision, the agent could follow it.

**Thea:** Certainly. We should not deny a connected agent what it can learn. We should ask where, in each case, the authority for acting comes from. For this launch, what would you need to know?

**Damon:** Who approved the review requirement, what work it applies to, and whether anyone later changed it.

**Thea:** Then the first task is not to connect a fourth tool. It is to establish those things.

## II. The storehouse

The next morning, Damon returned with a collection of notes.

**Damon:** We have put our company knowledge in one place. The launch plan is there. The security discussion is there. Yesterday's uncertainty need not be rediscovered tomorrow.

**Thea:** That is a gain. Show me what the new agent would read.

**Damon:** Here is LAUNCH NOW, copied from the project plan. Beneath it is REVIEW FIRST, copied from the discussion.

**Thea:** Two instructions preserved faithfully. Which one governs?

**Damon:** I can add a heading: Current decision. I can put REVIEW FIRST beneath it.

**Thea:** Good. And who may change that heading?

**Damon:** The project owner, after the review. Perhaps the agent can update it when it sees the decision.

**Thea:** Then your storehouse can carry the answer, if someone maintains it. What happens when a second project uses the same security review but has a different launch date?

**Damon:** A broad heading may mislead. We must say what the decision applies to.

**Thea:** And if an old note remains searchable?

**Damon:** We should mark it as replaced, not merely write another note nearby.

**Thea:** You see the distinction. Remembering both statements saves the next agent from hunting for them. It does not spare you from saying how one bears on the other.

**Damon:** I could do that with plain text.

**Thea:** You could. The difficulty is not the material of the storehouse. It is the labour of keeping its meaning current.

## III. The threads

A week later Damon drew three circles: the launch, the review decision, and the security report.

**Damon:** I have joined them. The launch depends on the review decision. The decision rests on the report. It replaces the earlier instruction to launch now.

**Thea:** Who made the replacement?

**Damon:** The project owner approved it. The link points to that approval.

**Thea:** Then you have given your agent more than a pile of notes. It can follow why the launch waits, and it can show the path to someone who doubts it.

**Damon:** We call this a knowledge graph.

**Thea:** Does the name require a special database?

**Damon:** No. These relationships could be maintained in files. What matters is that they are explicit and can be followed.

**Thea:** Is every line you draw true?

**Damon:** A line can be mistaken, just as a sentence can. We need to know who asserted it and on what evidence.

**Thea:** Look now at the project board.

**Damon:** It still says LAUNCH NOW.

**Thea:** Have the threads failed?

**Damon:** They have exposed another task. The shared account knows the decision, but the place where the team acts still disagrees. An agent could update the board.

**Thea:** And if it does not?

**Damon:** Someone must notice and reconcile the two. Otherwise the next agent may begin from the wrong place.

**Thea:** Thus the graph can make the relationship and its basis legible. It cannot make a separate copy agree merely by knowing that it should.

## IV. The place of work

Damon erased LAUNCH NOW from the board. Thea stopped him.

**Thea:** If you erase it, how will we know why the board changed?

**Damon:** I should keep the earlier instruction as history, marked as replaced. The active project view should show REVIEW FIRST.

**Thea:** Could the project view and the account of the decision read from the same record?

**Damon:** They could. Then changing the governing decision would change what both views show. An agent working on the launch would encounter the review requirement in the place where it works.

**Thea:** Would that end maintenance?

**Damon:** No. The security report still arrives from elsewhere. Someone may make a decision in a conversation and never record it. A relationship may be wrong. People may disagree about who has authority. And the agent must not turn every remark into an order.

**Thea:** Then what have you gained?

**Damon:** One less separate account of the launch to keep aligned. The work and the knowledge about the work can be maintained together. The remaining disagreements are easier to see.

**Thea:** And what would you ask of the next agent?

**Damon:** Before acting, show me the current instruction, what it replaces, who authorized it, and what remains uncertain.

**Thea:** Would every task need such ceremony?

**Damon:** No. Only when the answer changes what the agent may safely do.

**Thea:** Then perhaps the question was never how much the agent could see.

**Damon:** It was whether the place where we work can carry enough of what we know—and enough of how we know it—for the next action to make sense.

Thea looked once more at the board. It now read REVIEW FIRST. Beneath it was the earlier instruction, still visible, and the reason it no longer governed.

**Thea:** And when the review is complete?

**Damon:** Then we shall have another decision to maintain.
